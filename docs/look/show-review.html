Show Night

You watched the old show and liked the part that was pure entertainment: the referee robot, house robots coming out when they shouldn't, commentary playing along. You also remembered choosing power trains in the old PC game. This page lists what the show and those games did, what Roboteer already has, and what I recommend we build.

Two agents did the groundwork: one researched the show, its 2016 reboot, the licensed games and other robot builders (sources at the end); one mapped the game as it is on main today.

My recommendation

First wave: a proper show night

  1. The Marshal, our own neutral arena official, running the count-out you can see, breaking up stuck machines on camera and carding a house bot that oversteps.
  2. Walk-outs and a tale of the tape. The venue entrance is already built and never used. Add a name card with stats and a strength and weakness line you write yourself.
  3. Commentary with a personality, as text: hundreds of lines tied to real events, naming machines and parts, with mock outrage when a house bot misbehaves.
  4. House bot mischief, but only after the result: a finishing ritual on the loser, a late hit after the bell, the Marshal waving a card. Never anything that changes who won.
  5. The crowd reacts to the verdict, cheering or booing a judges' decision.
  6. Three bugs fixed (listed at the end).

Then the power train builder as its own wave, mockups first, because it changes how every machine drives and needs a full rebalance.

The show layer

Each idea as it worked on TV, where Roboteer stands, and what I recommend. Cost is my rough build size: S is a day, M a few days, L a week or more.

A referee robot

MissingCost MBuild now
On TV
A neutral robot that counted out immobile machines with a visible counter, put out fires, pulled stuck robots apart and showed cards to house robots that broke the rules. It was the straight man, and the audience loved it when it got hit. It was created so the villains never had to do anything kind.
Roboteer
The rules are there (10 second count, lock-up break) but nobody does them. The count is a HUD number and the lock-up break is an invisible shove.
Proposal
The Marshal: a hovering drone with a light bar, not a striped humanoid. It flies to a downed machine and counts with its lights, sprays out fires, tows stuck machines apart (the same push as today, now visible), shows the start lights and cards the house bot. It stays out of the physics, so it never changes a fight. It can get clipped by debris and wobble, for the laugh.

Start and stop calls

PartialCost SBuild now
On TV
A countdown everyone chanted, and a stop call. A team that missed the stop call and kept fighting was a story in itself.
Roboteer
3, 2, 1, FIGHT and a bell. No spoken stop.
Proposal
Our own words on the Marshal's lights (for example "3, 2, 1, FIGHT" then "POWER DOWN"). The show's own two words stay off limits.

House bots that misbehave

MissingCost MBuild now, after the result only
On TV
House robots kept to corner zones, then came out to finish off a beaten robot: carried it to the flames, the floor flipper or the pit. Sometimes they "broke the rules": hit after the stop call, ganged up, took revenge on a team that had damaged them. The commentator sold it all as their own anger. Much of it was staged.
Roboteer
Five house bots with corner zones, coming out after 15 quiet seconds and hunting at the endgame. They follow their rules exactly and freeze when the fight ends.
Proposal
After a knockout, the house bot drags or shoves the loser into the arena's hazard during the finish beat. Now and then, a late hit after the bell, the Marshal flashes a card and the commentary plays outraged. Fans' anger at the show was always about results being affected, so none of this ever touches the result.

House bots you can hurt, with a memory

MissingCost MNext
On TV
The first time a house robot was flipped was a headline. One was "killed" on the flame pit, given a mock funeral and rolled back on bandaged. One was famous for never being flipped. One competitor fought a years-long feud with one of them.
Roboteer
House bots can't be knocked out, self-right after 4 seconds and remember nothing.
Proposal
Damage you do to a house bot carries into its next appearance (patched plates, a limp). Flip one and it holds a grudge: it comes for you first next time and the commentary says so. A trophy part for beating each house bot on its home arena, like the gold parts in the handheld game.

A rampage night

MissingCost MNext
On TV
The house robots "rebelled": no zones, no referee, the arena openly rigged in their favour. The presenter announced it as a mutiny. Pure entertainment.
Roboteer
The code can already field a boss as an opponent (only QA uses it).
Proposal
A side event where two or three house bots roam free and the hazards fire on you. Survive the clock or topple one. Clearly flagged as unfair, which is the fun.

Commentary with a personality

PartialCost M (text)Build now as text
On TV
Football-style hyperbole, puns on robot names, names for moves, laughing at disasters, mock outrage at the house robots, predictions before a fight. It was recorded after the edit, so it always matched the action. The licensed games failed here: their lines called mobile robots immobile and blamed "poor driving" for knockouts.
Roboteer
Seven ticker lines and about twenty stage and payoff words, all capitals, never naming a machine. No voice (your call on 24 Sept).
Proposal
A few hundred original lines keyed to real fight events: names both machines, names the part that came off, calls a comeback, a stall, a disaster, a house bot late hit. A pre-fight prediction line. Every line checked against the event that fired it. A voiced announcer stays a separate decision (cost: a voice actor or on-device speech, plus app size).

Walk-outs and the tale of the tape

Built, never usedCost SBuild now
On TV
Every robot got an animated card: weight, size, speed, weapons, and a strength and weakness written by the team, often a joke ("Strengths: made of plastic. Weaknesses: made of plastic.") that the commentator read out. The reboot added gate entrances with hero shots.
Roboteer
The venue entrance (light sweep, gate chaser, pyro) exists but only a test flag fires it. Stats appear only on the scouting screen.
Proposal
Each machine rolls out of the gate with its card on screen, then the fight intro. Two free-text lines in the builder for your own strength and weakness. Rivals get theirs written for them.

A pit button

MissingCost MNext
On TV
A button on the arena wall opened the pit, with a siren. Pressing it was a statement of intent. The reboot's random dial version was disliked.
Roboteer
The Trapdoor pit opens on a timer and stays open from 1:30.
Proposal
A wall button in the Trapdoor: first machine to drive into it opens the pit early, with a siren and a camera cut. Always the same result, never random.

Pit crew between rounds

PartialCost S to MNext
On TV
Teams interviewed at their benches, a pit reporter checking damage, and the gamble of going out on a patched robot.
Roboteer
Damage carries over and repairs are a menu. Rivals have one grudge line each.
Proposal
A short pit scene before each career fight: your machine on the stand showing its real damage, a line from the opposing roboteer, repairs chosen right there. A rematch offer after a loss.

A victory moment

PartialCost MNext
On TV
Slow-motion replays of the deciding hit with commentary over them, and the winner's interview.
Roboteer
A 3.5 second finish beat with slow motion and a wreckage orbit. Replays exist, but only in the menu.
Proposal
Replay the deciding five seconds from a second camera at half speed, with a commentary line, then the winner under a spotlight with pyro. The replay system already re-runs fights exactly, so this is cheap to do well.

The crowd disagrees with the judges

PartialCost SBuild now
On TV
Verdicts were often booed on camera. The classic judges also scored "style". The reboot dropped it.
Roboteer
The judges were fixed on Monday: a 5, 3, 2 split with a card on the result screen.
Proposal
The crowd cheers a clear decision and boos a close one. No style score: the judges have only just been made fair, and a vague column would undo that.

Floor flipper, drop zone junk, fog

MissingCost MLater or skip
On TV
A floor panel that launched beaten robots, household junk dropped on them, and fog that hid the action. Fans disliked hazards that decided fights on their own, and the fog hid the fight from viewers too.
Proposal
A floor flipper as the house bot's finishing tool fits the ritual above. Skip the fog: on a phone screen it would just hide the game.

The power train builder

Today the drive is one bundled part: 2WD, 4WD, tracks and the rest each come with fixed speed, turn and push. There is no choice of motor, gearing, battery or wheel, no power budget, and top speed ignores how heavy your machine is. The physics already treats speed, pushing power and grip as separate numbers, so this builds on what's there.

Motor

Pick a character

  • Described in feel words, as the handheld game did: "fast but thirsty", "strong and steady", "slow and brutal"
  • Sets top speed, torque and power draw
Gearing

Speed against push

  • Three settings: sprint, balanced, grunt
  • Trades top speed for pushing power on the same motor
Battery

Capacity against weight

  • A light pack saves kilos for armour, but sags in the endgame
  • Weapons and boost draw from the same pack
  • Tuned so a sensible build never runs flat in 3 minutes
Wheels

Size and tyre

  • Bigger wheels: faster, more ground clearance, easier to get under
  • Grippy or hard tyres: push against turn
What the research says makes building fun

Parts must visibly change the fight. Two older games failed because they didn't. Show whole-machine numbers (acceleration, push against your real weight, run time), not the part's. Offer a quick build by target stats for players who don't want to tinker. Give events entry rules (a weight class, a weapon type) so building has a purpose.

Cost L. It changes how every machine drives, so it needs a full rebalance and new golden tests. I recommend builder mockups first so you can see it before anything changes, then build it as its own wave.

What we won't copy

The brief allows the format and forbids the branding. Ideas like a referee, corner zones, a pit and judges are generic. These stay out:

KindExamples
Show namesThe show's own name, its reboot, the US show
Characters and their shapesEvery house robot and the referee robot, by name and by silhouette (the striped humanoid referee, the tusked lifter, the knight, the tank, the scorpion)
Their wordsThe countdown and stop words, the presenter's and commentator's catchphrases
Their termsThe corner zone's name, the pit's branded name, the named hazards and event names
