App icon: plan on a grid, sinister

Mockups only; the app's icon is unchanged. The owner liked Plan on a grid from the blueprint round and asked for more versions that look more sinister. All eight keep what worked: the machine seen from above, the centre lines, a target, graph paper, drawn in bold, clean lines with at most one warning colour. The menace comes from darker grounds, meaner machines (a knife nose, a bar spinner, pincers, saw teeth, scarred armour) and targeting language (lock-on brackets, a radar, a strike zone). Nothing military, no guns and no insignia.

Every mark is drawn by hand as SVG, so all of them are clean vectors with an SVG master. Most now point their nose down, towards you, where the original pointed away. The lockups use a harder cut of the same drawn wordmark: the same single line weight, with chamfered corners and square ends instead of round ones. The original keeps its round lettering for comparison.

All eight and the original on a home screen, at 180 px and at real size (60 px)

Original: plan on a grid

The one the owner liked: the machine from above, centre lines, a target at its heart, white on blueprint blue graph paper.

Kept as the reference for this round, with the round lettering it was shown with.

1

Lock-on

Strong pick

Plan 7 made colder: near-black ground, a longer knife nose, and four red lock-on brackets closing round the machine with a red target at its heart.

Trade-off: the closest to what the owner liked and the safest; the menace is in the red brackets more than in the machine itself.

2

Spinner

A bar spinner seen from above: a long blade on a hub across the front, caught mid-turn, with an amber arc marking where it hits.

Trade-off: the weapon is unmistakable and the tilted bar feels dangerous, but it makes the icon wide and the hull becomes secondary.

3

Jaws

Two pincer arms reach forward from a squat hull and close on a red reticle, as if the machine has already chosen what it will grab.

Trade-off: the meanest shape of the eight, but pincers from above can read as a beetle or a crab before they read as a robot.

4

Radar

The grid turns into a radar screen: range rings, a sweep coming round, the machine at the centre and one red blip ahead of it.

Trade-off: the most atmospheric, but a radar is a borrowed sign and the machine has to be small to leave room for the screen.

5

Scarred

A heavy armoured shell with a chunk torn out of one shoulder and three red strike marks across it, a machine that has already won ugly.

Trade-off: the most story in one icon, but damage and slashes are the fussiest details, and at 60 px the tear is only a notch.

6

Lunge

Caught mid-lunge: the wedge turned hard towards the corner, three drag lines behind it and a red target where the nose is about to land.

Trade-off: the only one with movement, but the angle breaks the calm symmetry that made 7 look like a drawing.

7

Saw and eye

Strong pick

A wedge whose front edges are saw teeth, blades raked back off both flanks, and in the middle one red eye where the target used to be.

Trade-off: the most sinister, the eye does a lot of work; it edges towards a creature and away from an engineering drawing.

8

Kill zone

A targeting screen: the wedge low in the frame, a red fan of hatching ahead of it marking where it will strike, the target at the far edge.

Trade-off: the clearest story of threat, but the machine shrinks to make room for the fan, so the robot is less of the icon.

Two strongest: 1 Lock-on and 7 Saw and eye

  • 1 Lock-on is the original made colder rather than replaced. The machine is the same, the nose comes at you, and the red brackets and red target turn the drafting sheet into a targeting screen. It is the safest step from what you liked, and it reads at 60 px.
  • 7 Saw and eye is the most sinister: saw teeth down the nose, blades off both flanks and one red eye in the middle. It is still a plan view on a grid, but it has a presence the others don't.
  • Also worth a look: 3 Jaws if you want the machine itself to be the threat, and 5 Scarred if you want a veteran rather than a hunter.
