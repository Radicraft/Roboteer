App icon and logo: the 2D round

Mockups only; the app's icon is unchanged. After the first round (icon-options.html) the owner asked for 2D, so this round is eight flat directions, each a different idea, not one idea refined. Every mark is drawn by hand as SVG with a small, strong palette; no 3D, no metal, no bevels. Each has its SVG master next to it.

The lockups set the name in a face that suits each direction: Avenir Next, Futura, DIN and Barlow, or lettering drawn for the mark (5 and 7). Once a direction is chosen, the wordmark gets drawn properly as outlines. The marks are original shapes only, with no show names, house robot shapes or borrowed logos.

All eight on a home screen, at 180 px and at real size (60 px)
1

Launch

Two shapes and one moment: an orange wedge ramp has just thrown a ball into the air, which is the whole sport in one gesture.

Trade-off: it is abstract, so nobody will see a robot in it until they know the game, and a ramp and a ball could also suggest skate, sport or a physics toy.

2

Grin

Strong pick

A character you can root for: a squat black machine with narrowed eyes and an orange jaw plate full of bolts, fierce but friendly.

Trade-off: a face is the fastest read on a home screen and the easiest to love, but robot faces are a crowded look and it pulls the brand younger.

3

Tyre R

A monogram badge: a knobbly tyre seen side-on, with a tall condensed R standing in the hub where the wheel nut would be.

Trade-off: solid and ownable as a badge, but a letter in a circle is a familiar shape (clubs, garages, teams) and says 'wheels' more than 'fight'.

4

Decal

Strong pick

A die-cut workshop sticker slapped on a red toolbox: a stubby yellow wedge machine with a hazard band and a white cut border.

Trade-off: warm, fun and very 'garage', but the sticker border eats space at small sizes and the style dates faster than a plain mark.

5

Arcade

A 1980s arcade sprite: the wedge charging right in chunky pixels, with speed dashes behind it, like player one on an attract screen.

Trade-off: instantly charming and very readable small, but it promises a retro game, and Roboteer's fights are modern 3D physics.

6

Stencil

Spray-painted through a cut stencil onto an olive crate: the machine seen from above, with the stencil's bridges, soft overspray and one drip.

Trade-off: gritty and honest to the workshop, but the military stencil look is common in shooters and the texture muddies below 60 px.

7

Blueprint

One weight of white line on blueprint blue: the wedge drawn in isometric like a page from the build manual, the workshop side of the game.

Trade-off: calm, clever and clean, but the quietest icon of the eight: thin line work loses punch on a busy home screen.

8

Burst

A two-colour fight poster: the machine head-on in solid black, bursting out of a sunburst on hot orange, loud and graphic.

Trade-off: the most dramatic and the most 'fight night', but sunbursts are a stock poster device and the silhouette needs the burst to read.

Two strongest picks: 2 Grin and 4 Decal

  • 2 Grin is the fastest read of the eight at 60 px: a face is seen before anything else on a home screen, and the orange jaw is the wedge, so it is still our machine. It can also grow into a mascot for loading screens, stickers and store art.
  • 4 Decal says "you build this in a garage" with warmth, and the yellow and black machine on toolbox red stands out in any grid. The sticker idea carries into merchandise and in-game rewards.
  • Worth a second look: 7 Blueprint if you want the calmer, cleverer route, and 8 Burst if you want fight night.

The quickest way to choose: say which two or three you like, even for one detail, and the next round combines and refines those.
