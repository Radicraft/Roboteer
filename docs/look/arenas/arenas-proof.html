The Grinder

Two always-on floor grinders on the flanks. The concept: a working machine shop, cold steel and hot sparks, bleachers behind safety mesh under a gantry crane.

What was built

  • A shed round the arena: ribbed steel walls and a roof of trusses 15 m up (no sky), so it reads indoors.
  • Fluorescent tube banks hung on chains over the whole shed, cold white, one tube flickering; two real cold downlights over the arena with beam haze on high and rayed beam cards on medium.
  • Metal bleachers (the scaffold stand bays) on the south side behind a two-panel-high chain-link safety mesh, a sparse dark crowd on them, banners with no text, the screen.
  • The machine line down the east side: lathes, pillar presses and milling machines built from merged blocks in grey-green machine paint, pipe runs, a cable tray, and a mezzanine with a few people watching.
  • Hero piece: a yellow overhead gantry crane, box girder with a hazard stripe across the arena, runways on the shed walls, trolley, hoist, a hook block on four falls of chain and a beacon.
  • Floor: the Grinder's own riveted plate map (rivet rows along every seam, few welds, a little oil) with radial grind marks and bright filings round both wells, dark cool steel, the venue's own reflection probe.
  • Walls: x-braced steel panels (a new shared wall-panel shader) under a cold wash light.
  • Cold steel-blue grade: key and fill cold, blue-grey ambient and fog; the only warm colour is the sparks off the discs (the hazards lead's).

What is still short of the concept

  1. The sparks are not the concept's plume. The concept's hero is a constant spark fountain off each disc; ours is the hazards lead's small idle trickle, so the fight frame has no warm light at all until contact. A bigger idle plume is the hazards lead's call (their emitter), not the kit's.
  2. The floor is still darker and bluer than the concept's pale, bright steel, and the reflections are soft.
  3. The x-brace on the walls is painted, not embossed; at the fight camera it reads as a pattern, not as pressed steel.
  4. The machines are primitive blocks. They read as a machine line from the concept angle and are too plain up close.
  5. The tube fittings read as black slabs from the high reveal camera (you see their tops).
  6. The crowd is sparse by design (the concept shows a thin weeknight house) but at the fight camera nobody is in frame.
  7. Nothing measured on the phone.

Drop Zone

A central pit under a retracting cover. The concept: an underground bunker where the pit is the light source, amber beacons, haze, and a dark crowd that is mostly small lights. The frames below are shot with the pit OPEN (the capture waits for it).

The concept angle

The real fight camera

The venue

Medium and low tiers

What was built

  • A concrete bunker 44 m across with a dark ceiling and ribs, so the arena is underground, not in a void.
  • Steep concrete stands on all four sides behind a 1.4 m front wall (a new shared stepped-stand piece): the front rows are MakeHuman people, the rest silhouettes; phones on three people in ten, rows of small lamps along the landings.
  • Four cold downlights from the ceiling corners with volumetric haze on high and rayed cone cards on medium; a dark rig hanging over the pit with a ring of cold tubes under it.
  • Four amber beacons on posts at the arena's outside corners, turning slowly (a new shared rotating-beacon piece). Idle only: the warning stays the hazards lead's rim strip and lamps.
  • While the pit is open (the kit reads pit_open(), never writes it): embers rising out of the shaft and, on high, a red haze inside it.
  • Floor: the Drop Zone's own concrete bay map (joints, damp, old oil, tyre marks), nothing inside the pit rim; a cold, dark grade where amber and the pit red are the only warm colours.

What is still short of the concept

  1. The open pit still reads as a flat red square from above, not the concept's deep glowing shaft with haze pouring out. The shaft's walls and glow are the hazards lead's; the kit's red haze and embers sit inside it and barely show from the fight camera.
  2. The floor is darker and bluer than the concept's grey concrete, and the approach stripes are thin paint lines, not the concept's wide yellow bars.
  3. The beacons are small idle props at the corners; the concept has four big lamp posts at the pit's corners throwing amber beams across the floor. Posts inside the arena would stand in the machines' way, so they are outside the wall.
  4. The crowd reads as silhouettes and phones, as the concept asks, but the rim light on every head makes it busier than the concept's quiet dark house.
  5. The downlight cones are strong from the floor (the stand frame) and invisible from the fight camera.
  6. Nothing measured on the phone.

Furnace

Wall flame jets and a conveyor. The concept: a foundry with molten metal behind the walls, catwalk stands lit orange from below, chains and hooks, embers and smoke.

The concept angle

The real fight camera

The venue

Medium and low tiers

What was built

  • A foundry hall 48 m across with dark steel walls, rust columns, roof girders and, on high, a band of smoke hanging at 7 to 14 m (a fog volume, clear over the floor).
  • Catwalk stands round the south and east walls at 3.3 m and 4.5 m: grating decks on rust columns with cross braces and rails, the crowd two rows deep on each, lit orange from below by two uplights; a scaffold stair at each end.
  • Hero piece: a crucible hung from a yellow hook frame, tipped, pouring a molten stream into a mould on a plinth beyond the south stand; the metal is an emissive crawl (a new molten shader) that throws its own orange light and spits embers. A second, distant pour for the reveal.
  • Chains and hooks from the roof over the stands and one over the arena, all high above the machines.
  • Embers drifting through the whole room.
  • Floor: the Furnace's own riveted plate map with slag drips; rusted x-braced wall panels; a warm key from above and a deep orange fill from low, dark steel above.

What is still short of the concept

  1. The crucible does not yet read as molten from most angles: the ladle's surface is small and the pour is a thin line. It needs a bigger, brighter pour, glow haze round it and a heat shimmer, and to sit where the concept angle sees it.
  2. The flame jets are the concept's hero and they are the hazards lead's; they are not firing in these frames, so the orange that should dominate the concept angle is missing.
  3. The hall beyond the catwalks falls to black; the concept shows a lit, busy foundry (other furnaces, cranes, fire) far behind.
  4. No heat haze over the floor (the concept brief asks for it on high).
  5. The rust and wall panels are painted patterns, not the concept's heavy riveted steel with depth.
  6. Nothing measured on the phone.

The Crush

Two wall rams that pin and flatten. The concept: a hydraulic press hall with oppressive scale, concrete stands behind thick safety glass, cold white work light on pale concrete, hazard yellow the only saturated colour.

The concept angle

The real fight camera

The venue

Medium and low tiers

What was built

  • A press hall 44 m across: concrete walls and columns, a low dark concrete ceiling on beams, a grid of square work lights under it and two real cold downlights over the arena (haze on high, cone cards on medium).
  • Concrete stands on all four sides behind a 1.9 m front wall and a 2.2 m run of safety glass on galvanised posts, the crowd stepping up behind it (front rows people, back rows silhouettes).
  • Hero piece: a press frame behind each ram wall. Two big hydraulic cylinders with chrome rods and collars, a hazard-striped crosshead with a work light under it, a manifold with hoses running down to the ram, pressure gauges facing the arena, and a beacon on top (idle; the warning is the hazards lead's).
  • Pipe runs under the ceiling and down four columns, steam venting from the joints.
  • Floor: the Crush's own pale concrete bay map with branching cracks (a new crack pass in the floor generator), dust and a few tyre marks; cold, near-white grade.

What is still short of the concept

  1. The press frames are big, dark and simple from the concept angle; the east one sits between the camera and the east ram and reads as a black lump rather than machinery. The concept's presses are chrome and detailed, and do not hide the ram.
  2. No dust burst, crack decal or camera kick on a press from the kit (those are the hazards lead's cues in the shared language); the concept's defining image, dust rolling out as a ram pins a machine, is not in these frames.
  3. The floor is paler but the concept's bright, near-white concrete with deep crack shadows is still brighter and harsher than ours.
  4. Steam is faint and only reads where a light catches it.
  5. The glass reads as a crisp outline over the crowd, not the concept's thick, slightly green safety glass.
  6. Nothing measured on the phone.

Coliseum

The championship arena: every hazard plus the turntables, 12 m square. The concept: the stadium final, a full house on all four sides, a truss full of lights, a jumbotron, a polished dark floor that mirrors the show, blue and red with gold.

The concept angle

The real fight camera

The venue

Medium and low tiers

What was built

  • A full bowl on all four sides: a 2.6 m banner wall round the arena, then 16 rows up on the far sides and 12 on the near sides; the front rows MakeHuman people, the upper bowl silhouettes; phones on three people in ten and more camera flashes than anywhere else; gold lamps along three landings.
  • Red and blue banners hanging down the banner wall with pointed feet, a gold trim rail along its top.
  • Hero piece: a square truss ring 18 m across flown at 10 m (no floor towers, so it never blocks a camera) with twelve moving heads sweeping blue and red, and a four-faced jumbotron hanging inside it; one face shows the live fight, the other three loop the venue's stings (only one live feed per venue, it renders the scene again).
  • Four real spots from the truss (blue, red, white, gold) with haze on high and cone cards on medium.
  • Floor: a polished dark plate map, clean round the turntables, grinder wells and spike patch, metallic and glossy so it mirrors the lights through the venue's reflection probe and SSR; dark blue padded wall panels.
  • The endgame pyro columns in all four corners come from the kit's shared 'columns' group.

What is still short of the concept

  1. The house is not yet the concept's wall of people: the bowl is two thirds as tall as the concept's and the near stands crowd the corners of the concept angle.
  2. The banners read as separate dark panels, not the concept's continuous, bright red and blue curtain.
  3. The truss ring cuts across the wide and reveal frames; the concept's truss runs along the far edge of the view.
  4. The floor mirrors the light but not yet the crowd and the pyro as sharply as the concept (SSR only on high, the probe is baked once).
  5. No confetti on the result and no stand leaning toward each machine's livery (both in the concept brief).
  6. Low tier draws a lot of calls here (the biggest venue): 346 against 368 without it on Compatibility, so the venue itself is cheap there, but the arena is the heaviest in the game.
  7. Nothing measured on the phone.
