Medium and low tiers

Medium: --rendering-method mobile, two real flood lights, 60 percent of the cards. Low: --rendering-method gl_compatibility, no kit lights, 30 percent of the cards, no silhouettes, no phones, no beams, no dust (the night grade is lifted a little on low so the floor still reads).

What is still short of the concept

Blunt, most visible first.

  1. The fight camera will never look like the concept. The concept is a lower, wider shot. At pitch 50 with the 28 degree lens the crowd reaches only the top corners of the frame, and only when the fight is within about 1.5 m of the east or south wall. Most of a fight is floor. The concept's view exists in the game only at the entrance, the endgame reveal and the KO orbit.
  2. The floor is darker and dirtier, but it is still the same plate texture. The square bolt holes and the 1 m seam grid still read as tiles. The concept has big welded plates, a strong wet sheen that catches the floods, and heavy tyre marks. Our oil is glossy, but the reflections have almost nothing bright to show, and the tyre marks are faint at the fight camera. Next step: a Yard-specific plate set (larger plates, no bolt grid) and brighter tyre marks.
  3. The light has the right colours but not the concept's contrast. A warm key and a cold rim are there, and the machines read well. The concept's hot pools against black corners are softer here, because the floods are two unshadowed spots plus a painted-in pool. The flood towers themselves are out of the concept-angle frame.
  4. The walls are dark boxes with a painted sleeve. The concept's walls are pale, lit concrete with thick worn stripes on the top and the inner face. Ours have the stripes, but the inner faces turned away from the key go almost black, and the chipping is procedural noise.
  5. The crowd is real people now, but it is flat and dark. Under the night grade most bodies go close to black and the clothing colours vanish. Faces are only a hint at 60 to 100 px. The cards are flat, which shows when the camera orbits. Phone screens read as white rectangles rather than the concept's sparkle. The concept stacks the crowd on containers directly behind the far wall; ours has a standing row at the wall and the containers 2.5 m back.
  6. The spike patch is not the concept's ring of bulbs. The shared warning (hazards lead, one warning language, 1.25 s) lights a bezel with lamp dashes and pulses it from amber to red; it does not fill bulb by bulb over 1.5 s. Left as it is on purpose: one warning language is your decision, and a Yard-only bulb ring would be a second language.
  7. Dust and grit are subtle. Motes in the light and a few streaks of grit on the wind, plus one flickering flood. There is no lit haze in the beams (beam cards are faint on purpose: the first pass drew grey fog discs).
  8. Low tier is very dark. 80 cards, no phones, no silhouettes, no beams. It reads as a night venue but thin.
  9. Nothing here was measured on the phone. All numbers below are desktop.

New textures: one 2048 x 2048 floor map, VRAM compressed with mipmaps. Load: the Yard is now stood up once inside the loading screen's warm-up, so its pipelines compile there. With the shader cache cleared, the Yard's first eight frames took 1040 ms cold (worst frame 848 ms) and 287 ms after the warm-up (worst 196 ms); the warm-up itself is about 2 s behind the loading screen on desktop.

What changed

  • Night grade (VenueKit.grade, per venue): sodium key from the east flood tower, a cold blue rim from across the arena, a darker blue ambient and fog. It only sets render properties of the arena's lights, environment and meshes, once, after the tier is applied.
  • Floor: the venue floor shader over the same plate textures, multiplied by one unique 10.4 m map: oil pools (wet and glossy), tyre skids and doughnuts, weld seams with heat tint, grime along the wall feet, a clean spike patch, and a painted light pool for the tiers without flood lights. The 1 m seam grid is darkened.
  • Wall tops: a worn hazard sleeve (45 degree bands, paint chipped to concrete, dirt down the face) replaces the thin chevron cap.
  • LED ring removed from the Yard; painted boards dimmed to 42 percent so nothing outshines the arena.
  • Beams end in the air and fade near the camera (they drew grey fog discs on the floor); moving-head beams halved.
  • Grit drifting on the wind (70 streaks on high, 30 on medium, none on low).
  • Crowd: MakeHuman atlas by default, a warm rim from the floods, darker bodies, a front row pressed to the east wall so heads reach the top of the fight frame.
  • Warm-up: Arena/render/warmup.gd stands the dressed Yard up once, shrunk, in its off-screen viewport.
