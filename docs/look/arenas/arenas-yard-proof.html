Pass 1

What is still short of the concept (after pass 2)

Blunt, most visible first. What pass 2 closed is at the end.

  1. The fight camera will never look like the concept. The concept is a lower, wider shot. At pitch 50 with the 28 degree lens the crowd reaches only the top corners of the frame, and only within about 1.5 m of the east or south wall. Most of a fight is floor. The concept's view exists in the game only at the entrance, the reveal and the KO orbit.
  2. The fight-camera floor is still warmer and flatter than the concept. The plates, seams, welds and tyre skids now read, and the wet oil shows a sodium sheen at the concept angle. But at the fight camera the steel sits in a dark warm brown, the cold pool is faint, and the sheen only shows on the patches that face the glint light. The concept's cool blue-grey steel with hot reflections needs a brighter, cooler reflection source (a baked probe of the lit venue) rather than more tint.
  3. The oil pools read as black holes in close frames. Glossy, but they reflect the dark sky. Fine at the concept angle, heavy in the closer fight framing.
  4. The beams are haze near the lamps, not crisp rays. On high the flood cones glow in the air over the stands; they are broad and soft, and the concept's sharp shafts with dust in them are not there. Medium uses cone cards, which read as flat glows. Low has none.
  5. The walls are pale now, but they are the concrete texture. The inner faces are lit by a wall-only wash, so they read as worn concrete, but the grain is a speckled granite at this scale, and there are no stains or scuffs from machines hitting them.
  6. The crowd is still cards. Real people (your pick A), more clothing colour, phones as small warm glints; but faces are only a hint at 60 to 100 px, the cards turn flat when the camera orbits, and the concept stacks the crowd on containers right behind the far wall while ours stands at the wall with containers 2.5 m back.
  7. The grit is denser but reads as sparse streaks, closer to light rain than to dust in a beam.
  8. Low tier is thin: 80 cards, no phones, no beams, a warmer and brighter floor than the other tiers.
  9. Nothing here was measured on the phone. All numbers below are desktop.

Closed by pass 2

  • The tile grid: the arena's 1 m seam grid is hidden in the Yard and the floor is the Yard's own irregular steel plates (2 to 3.4 m), with seams, weld beads, a few corner bolts and its own normal map.
  • Wet sheen: dark metallic steel, glossy where oily, and a floor-only sodium glint light from across the arena.
  • Tyre marks: heavy black skids and doughnuts, visible at the fight camera.
  • Near-black wall faces: pale worn concrete under a wall-only wash.
  • Beam haze: per-flood volumetric haze on high, stronger cone cards on medium; grit nearly doubled.
  • Phones: no more white rectangles; small warm-white points that glint.

With the shader cache cleared, the Yard's first eight frames took 1040 ms cold (worst frame 848 ms) and 287 ms after the warm-up (worst 196 ms); the warm-up itself is about 2 s behind the loading screen on desktop.

What changed

  • Night grade (VenueKit.grade, per venue): sodium key from the east flood tower, a cold blue rim from across the arena, a darker blue ambient and fog. It only sets render properties of the arena's lights, environment and meshes, once, after the tier is applied.
  • Floor: the venue floor shader over the same plate textures, multiplied by one unique 10.4 m map: oil pools (wet and glossy), tyre skids and doughnuts, weld seams with heat tint, grime along the wall feet, a clean spike patch, and a painted light pool for the tiers without flood lights. The 1 m seam grid is darkened.
  • Wall tops: a worn hazard sleeve (45 degree bands, paint chipped to concrete, dirt down the face) replaces the thin chevron cap.
  • LED ring removed from the Yard; painted boards dimmed to 42 percent so nothing outshines the arena.
  • Beams end in the air and fade near the camera (they drew grey fog discs on the floor); moving-head beams halved.
  • Grit drifting on the wind (70 streaks on high, 30 on medium, none on low).
  • Crowd: MakeHuman atlas by default, a warm rim from the floods, darker bodies, a front row pressed to the east wall so heads reach the top of the fight frame.
  • Warm-up: Arena/render/warmup.gd stands the dressed Yard up once, shrunk, in its off-screen viewport.

Pass 2

  • Floor: the Yard's own plate map (irregular plates, seams, welds, corner bolts, heavy tyre skids, oil, damp) with a normal map; the arena's scratched plate only adds fine scratches at low contrast; dark metallic steel; painted warm and cold pools; the arena's 1 m grid hidden in the Yard.
  • Sheen: a sodium glint light from across the arena that only the floor sees, so the wet plates light up toward the camera without moving a shadow.
  • Walls: pale concrete tint and a wall-only wash from the camera side.
  • Beams: a volumetric fog volume along the first metres of each real flood (high); stronger cone cards (medium).
  • Crowd: phones drawn as small warm-white glints that flicker, not lit rectangles; clothing saturation and body light up a little; crowd atlases now have mipmaps (less shimmer).
