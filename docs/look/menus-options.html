Roboteer: the career screen and the menu, five ways

Design brief: a screen has one job and one hero. The career screen must answer three questions in one glance: where am I, who is next and what is at stake, what do I press. At most two boxed panels per screen; everything else is plain type on the glass. Today's screen has sixteen identical panels and answers none of the three quickly.

Option A · The card
One panel, and FIGHT lives inside it. Everything about the next tie sits in one card: who, what they drive, where, who guards the corner, the money as one sentence with only the numbers coloured. The tier strip is a line of five words, not five boxes. Your season becomes a four-slot strip under the card, so the bracket is on screen without competing.
  • Best answer to "what do I press": there is exactly one bright thing.
  • Garage and Repair are demoted to links, which is what they are.
  • Risk: the bracket strip is small. Fine for a bracket of 8 read left to right; tap it for the full board.
My recommendation. It is the least the screen can be while still answering all three questions.

Option B · The board
The bracket is the hero. A real tree of 8 with your path lit, your losses struck through, the next tie glowing. The next fight is a lower third under it and FIGHT sits beside it.
  • Sells the season most strongly. You see where you came from and what the final means.
  • Answers "who is next" a beat slower: the eye has to find the glowing cell.
  • Costs the most screen and text at phone size. Names in the first column are 11 pt.
This is the screen the brief described, drawn honestly. It is a good screen for a tournament game and a busy one on a 6-inch phone.

Option C · The broadcast
No panels at all. The machine owns the whole stage. The next fight is a single lower third, the way a live feed introduces a bout, with one FIGHT beside it. The season lives in the ticker and behind a "Season board" link.
  • The most cinematic and the closest to the board you chose. Zero clutter.
  • Answers "what do I press" instantly, "who is next" instantly, "where am I" only in the ticker and the tier line.
  • Risk: the bracket is one tap away instead of on screen. If the season feeling matters more than the stage, pick A or B.
Right for a game that wants every screen to feel like the arena. Weakest for players who want to study the tournament.

Option M1 · Career is the hero
One card says what happens next and one button continues it. Quick Fight, Workshop and Events become a plain list; Ladder, Replays and Settings stay small.
  • A returning player is one tap from the fight that matters.
  • Four equal buttons become one decision and three alternatives.
  • Risk: a brand new player has no career yet; the card then reads "Start your career" and the list stays.
My recommendation.

Option M2 · Today's menu, ranked
Keep the four buttons but make one of them primary. The smallest change: CAREER gets the primary treatment and the other three drop to secondary weight.
  • Cheapest to build and nothing moves.
  • Still four boxes; the "what's next" story stays in the ticker only.
Pick this if you like the menu as it is and only want the hierarchy fixed.

Rules I would write into the design system after this: one primary per screen; at most two boxed panels; money is a sentence, not three bugs; navigation that is not the hero is a text link; the tier strip is a line, never boxes.
