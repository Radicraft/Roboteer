Roboteer: the three post-match screens, two ways each

Brief v5 sections 5, 6 and 9.4. The order after every career match is Match Report, then Rewards on a win or the Retry prompt on a loss or a draw, then the Series screen. Design-system section 8 holds on all three: one hero, at most two boxed panels, money as a sentence with only the numbers coloured, navigation that is not the hero is a text link. The machine stands live on the left of every stage screen (28 % across), so chrome that covers the left 45 % is chrome the owner cannot see his own robot through.

All six frames are 874 x 402 pt, the real landscape phone, drawn in the Broadcast tokens. Fixture: Series 2, match 3, Tinribs against Clatterbox, 2:14, won by knockout, base 150 plus damage 118 plus survivor 75, 450 Level points, Level 13 crossing into 14.

Option R1 · The sheet, machine visible
One panel, the tally as two plain lines beside the banner, the condition list in two columns. The result, the clock and the hits read left to right in one glance; the panel is only the part-by-part truth, which is the one thing that needs a grid.
Two reasons to pick it: the owner's own machine stays on screen, battered, while he reads what is broken off it, and it is the same shape as the career card he chose (one panel, hero inside it, everything else plain type).

Option R2 · The two-panel tally sheet
Condition on the left, the fight's numbers on the right, the banner full width above them. The most room for a long part list and the easiest thing to read if a machine ever carries fifteen parts.
Two reasons not to: it uses both of the screen's allowed panels on one factual screen and hides the machine completely, and the hero button ends up outside everything, floating bottom right, which section 8 warns against.

Option W1 · The ledger
One panel: the four scrap lines counting up to a banked total, then the Level bar filling under it, then what opened. The rows are plain type on the glass with only the numbers in scrap, so it reads as a ledger and not as a row of score bugs.
Two reasons to pick it: the two tracks the brief is built on (Scrap, then Level) are stacked in the order the brief names them, and it is the same panel the Match Report just used, so the win is two beats of one screen rather than two unrelated screens.

Option W2 · The big number
No panel at all: the banked total is 64 pt of scrap over the stage, the bonus lines are one sentence, and the Level bar runs the full width of the screen above the ticker. The most cinematic of the six frames and the closest to a broadcast prize graphic.
Two reasons not to: the one big number sits exactly where the machine stands, so the payoff covers the robot that earned it, and with no panel the unlock lower third and the button float in the picture with nothing to sit in. It is also the loudest possible answer to a brief that asks for a tally.

Option T1 · The one question
No panel: the losing banner, three short lines, and the two answers stacked on the right with the garage as a link. The Match Report has just shown every number, so this screen exists to ask one question and it asks it in nine words.
Two reasons to pick it: a loss screen that boxes itself up feels like a penalty notice, and keeping Retry as the only primary with Repair-and-retry a step quieter is the honest hierarchy, since repairing is the same action with a bill attached.

Option T2 · The card again
The same card the career screen uses, filled in with who beat you and what it costs to go again, Retry inside it. Most consistent with the screen the owner already chose, and it re-states the opponent for a player who put the phone down.
Two reasons not to: repairing becomes a text link, which section 8 reserves for navigation rather than for an action that spends scrap and changes the fight, and the card repeats the opponent details the Match Report and the Series screen both already carry.

What all six share, and what I will build: lower thirds enter from the edge they belong to; every number that is money counts over 0.4 s and is coloured (scrap in, danger out) while the words stay white; the Level bar is a 3 pt rule that fills, never a pill; no exclamation marks, no em dashes, and the words tier, circuit, season and bracket appear nowhere. Recommended set: R1 + W1 + T1.
