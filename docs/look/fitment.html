1. Spinner rings: drawn bigger, round the machine

The ring's bore is now sized to the machine it is on: everything drawn inside the ring's height band (hull, wheels or tracks, deck parts, srimech, and the armour on a hull that keeps its bodywork) stands 12 mm inside it at every angle. The ring model is pushed out along its radius, band and teeth unchanged; the vortex's spider (its arms and hub) is left out, because the hull now fills the bore. On the Shell and the Apex, whose plates leave the collision world while the ring is up, the plates are narrowed into the bore instead. A raised ring was measured and dropped: the wheels reach 0.11 m above the axle plane, so a ring clear of them would have worked 0.12 m above its collider.

Physics change A vortex on any hull but the Shell used to spin about the hull's FRONT socket: the collider was a disc centred on the nose while the ring was drawn round the middle (on the Apex, 0.425 m apart). It now spins about the hull's centre on every hull. And the ring's collision disc grows with the drawn teeth, so a blow lands where you see it: its radius goes from the catalogue 0.45 (full body) and 0.48 m (vortex) to about 0.64 m on the Shell, 0.66 on the box, 0.69 on the wedge, 0.72 on the pyramid, 0.73 on the Tall Tank and 0.83 m on the Long and Low (4wd; tracks make it wider). The catalogue reach, which the shop shows and the AI drives to, is unchanged. Balance below.

2. Drum: out in front of the nose. Bar spinner: not changed

The drum's leading edge stood 30 mm proud of the armour, which left its back 90 to 170 mm inside the nose. It is now carried forward on its forks until its drawn teeth clear the drawn nose and front plate by 8 mm, and it runs at the floor there. Raising it instead needed 0.15 to 0.40 m of lift: not a drum any more. Physics change every drum fight (the drum stands 0.13 to 0.22 m further out). Out in front it took the ram's blows the hull used to take, as the saw did in wave C, so its catalogue row now carries part_toughness: 2.0.

The bar spinner is left as it was, on evidence. Its 1.1 m bar turns about the nose, so its circle covers the whole front half of the machine; clearing that needs the bar raised 0.22 to 0.26 m on the box, pyramid, apex and wedge and 0.43 m on the Tall Tank, which puts its underside 0.33 to 0.53 m off the floor, over the deck of every low machine in the game. A box bar raised 0.26 m in the gauntlet: 13–5 against 14–4, but half the damage (2.57 against 4.53 a fight), and it would miss a Long and Low or a wedge's nose outright. Shortening does not help either (it would have to be shorter than the distance to its own nose). The honest options are a different mount (a bar on a mast ahead of the nose) or accepting that a machine's own bar passes over its deck, which the physics already ignores. --tune=horizontal_spinner.fit_lift= stays in as a measuring aid.

3. Flippers and lifters on flat noses: a drawn hinge in front of the nose

On a flat nose the physics hinge sits inside the hull (the Apex's 83 mm behind its face), so the drawn arm's root was buried at rest and swung up through the front plate on every stroke. A limited stroke does not help: the Box's arm meets its plate in the first 4 degrees. So the DRAWN hinge now stands in front of the nose, far enough on each hull that the drawn arm clears the hull and the plate over the whole stroke, and the drawn arm turns about it by exactly the angle the physics arm has turned (a presentation node, DrawnHinge, like the axe's wind-up). The lifter's screw actuator is split off the forks and stays on the hull. Physics untouched. The wedge and the Long and Low keep their signed-off ramp fit.

4. Axe and crusher: the towers stand on the deck

The axe's tower and the crusher's tower were part of the moving arm, so they leaned with it: the axe's 33 degrees back through the front plate and the deck at rest, the crusher's 48 degrees back as its jaw opened. They are now their own node in each model (tower, pin, spring return; the crusher's anvil bed and ram cylinder too), stood upright on the deck under the hinge. The front plate is drawn with a notch under the crusher's bed. Only the arm and the jaw move. Physics untouched.

5. The pyramid's own side plates

New models, plate_pyramid_sides (steel, HDPE and reactive): armour tiles cut to the pyramid's six-sided flank, in two bands either side of the axle bosses, seven across. Any tile a wheel or a track stands over is left out, so the flank shows armour between and above the wheels instead of a flat plate standing 45 to 99 mm off the slopes. Visual only.

6. Scoop with a front weapon: the weapon wins

The scoop is scaled about the middle of its back edge on the floor, lower first, narrower second, shorter last, until nothing of it comes within 10 mm of anything the weapon draws over its stroke (a spinner's whole turn, an arm's whole swing, the mounts as they stand). Where nothing fits (the Long and Low's flipper rests with its tip on the floor line) the drawn scoop is left out and the front plate's box is still its collision. With a ring, the scoop is pulled into the bore. Visual only.

7. A fitted reactive lid for the wedge

New model, plate_wedge_top_reactive: the steel lid's shoulder pods, with twelve reactive cassettes on the pods' armour facets where the steel lid has its upper pressing. It replaces the flat cassette plate that sat over the pods like a lid; deck parts sit in the channel again, as they do under steel. 14.1k triangles, only on a wedge with reactive top armour. Visual only.

The owner's two cases

The spare battery floating in mid air

It was the hero wedge. Deck parts went on the hull model's deck socket, 0.224 m up, and the hull rework lowered the wedge's deck under it: 99 mm of air. On the box and four other hulls the same socket put the battery inside the srimech's base, and a second deck part went to the belly socket, inside the hull. Deck parts now take two slots either side of the srimech from Arena/data/fitment.json, their height measured off the drawn hull and armour. On the wedge that is the channel between the pods, behind the flipper's gas bottle; under reactive top armour (a flat plate over the whole deck) they sit on the plate.

The saw coming through the front

Two causes. The saw model was one piece, so its guard, belt covers and motor were bolted to the spinning blade and went round with it, through the nose and out of the deck. And the blade's centre sat inside the hull on every chassis, 125 mm inside on the Long and Low. The model is now split: the disc spins, the mount stays on the hull. The blade stands in front of the armour with its back 20 mm in a slot cut in the front plate.

Physics change Moving the blade forward changes every fight with a saw. The balance check further down has the numbers.

Tinribs, close up

The flipper was drawn 0.524 m wide, wider than the gap between the front tyres, so a tyre sat on its edge. Drawn at 6.5° it also stood 9 to 30 mm off the ramp, which read as a loose sheet over a second plate. The drawn flipper and front plate are now narrowed to clear the tyres by 30 mm, and the arm is laid on the ramp. The flipper's collision box, hinge and angle are untouched, so the fight is the same.

Out in front of the armour the saw blade took the ram's blows the hull used to take, and the ram knocked it off in six of nine fights. The saw's catalogue row now carries part_toughness: 2.0, which doubles its blade's break thresholds and halves the direct damage it takes.
