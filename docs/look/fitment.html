The owner's two cases

The spare battery floating in mid air

It was the hero wedge. Deck parts went on the hull model's deck socket, 0.224 m up, and the hull rework lowered the wedge's deck under it: 99 mm of air. On the box and four other hulls the same socket put the battery inside the srimech's base, and a second deck part went to the belly socket, inside the hull. Deck parts now take two slots either side of the srimech from Arena/data/fitment.json, their height measured off the drawn hull and armour. On the wedge that is the channel between the pods, behind the flipper's gas bottle; under reactive top armour (a flat plate over the whole deck) they sit on the plate.

The saw coming through the front

Two causes. The saw model was one piece, so its guard, belt covers and motor were bolted to the spinning blade and went round with it, through the nose and out of the deck. And the blade's centre sat inside the hull on every chassis, 125 mm inside on the Long and Low. The model is now split: the disc spins, the mount stays on the hull. The blade stands in front of the armour with its back 20 mm in a slot cut in the front plate.

Physics change Moving the blade forward changes every fight with a saw. The balance check further down has the numbers.

Tinribs, close up

The flipper was drawn 0.524 m wide, wider than the gap between the front tyres, so a tyre sat on its edge. Drawn at 6.5° it also stood 9 to 30 mm off the ramp, which read as a loose sheet over a second plate. The drawn flipper and front plate are now narrowed to clear the tyres by 30 mm, and the arm is laid on the ramp. The flipper's collision box, hinge and angle are untouched, so the fight is the same.

Out in front of the armour the saw blade took the ram's blows the hull used to take, and the ram knocked it off in six of nine fights. The saw's catalogue row now carries part_toughness: 2.0, which doubles its blade's break thresholds and halves the direct damage it takes. That put it back where it was (15–3, 3.43 damage a fight against 3.60). A shallower slot did not help (4–5 against the ram at 60 and 100 mm). The twin saw is unchanged and in the noise; it was already the top of the table. Nine fights a side is noisy: differences under three wins in nine mean nothing.

Left flagged on purpose

The scoop's back half sits behind the front plate, inside the nose (its lip is out in front, on the floor). The rear wedge is bolted into the tail. The shell's wheels stand 20 to 28 mm off its narrow hull, under the ring. The Long and Low's flipper tip rests 21 mm below the floor line at thickness 1, from its signed-off rest angle. The axe and crusher posts leave 6 mm of air. None of these read wrong in the renders.
