App icon: plan on a grid, round two

Mockups only; the app's icon is unchanged. The last round (icon-options-plan.html) moved too far from the original, so this round keeps the original drawing exactly: the same blueprint-blue graph paper, the same machine from above, the same centre lines crossing in the target, the same composition, pointing the same way, and the same white single-weight line. Each variant changes one thing, so the menace comes from a small edit. Variant 9 combines the two strongest edits.

The original was redrawn by hand as clean SVG lines, so every variant is a controlled edit of the same file. The lockups keep the original round lettering; the harder chamfered cut is shown once, under variant 9, as an alternative.

The original

Plan on a grid as the owner liked it, redrawn by hand as clean lines so every variant below starts from exactly the same drawing.

The baseline for this round.

1

Blade

Strong pick

The wedge becomes a solid white blade that runs past the hull to a longer point; nothing else changes.

Trade-off: the strongest change of silhouette, and still the original, but a filled shape breaks the all-line drawing a little.

2

Teeth

A row of small saw teeth along both edges of the wedge, so the nose looks like it bites.

Trade-off: menacing up close, but the teeth are the first detail to blur at 60 px, where they become a rough edge.

3

Red eye

Strong pick

The target in the middle turns into one red eye, the only colour on the page.

Trade-off: the smallest edit with the biggest effect; it does make the machine a creature, which some will find less technical.

4

Dark sheet

The same drawing on a deeper blueprint blue that darkens towards the edges, like a print under a single lamp.

Trade-off: moodier without touching the machine, but on its own it is the quietest of the eight.

5

Heavy line

Every line drawn at a heavier weight with sharp mitred corners, so the machine looks armoured and blunt.

Trade-off: reads best of all at 60 px, but the heavier line loses some of the fine drafting feel.

6

Slit wheels

The four round wheels become narrow tyre slits seen from above, which makes the silhouette tighter and meaner.

Trade-off: more accurate as a plan view and less friendly, but the round wheels were a big part of what made the original charming.

7

Charging

The whole machine turned a few degrees and leaning, with a faint shadow behind it, as if it has just started to charge.

Trade-off: the only one with movement; the tilt costs the calm, centred symmetry of the original.

8

Strike marks

Three red strike marks gouged across the top corner of the hull, the machine carrying the scars of its last fight.

Trade-off: tells a story in one detail, but claw marks are a familiar device and they sit on top of the drawing rather than in it.

1+3

Blade and red eye

The two strongest edits together: the solid white blade and the single red eye.

Trade-off: the most sinister that is still clearly the original; two edits is the limit before it becomes a new design.

Alternative lettering: the harder chamfered cut, shown once

Two strongest edits: 1 Blade and 3 Red eye (together in 9)

  • 1 Blade changes the silhouette without adding anything: the wedge becomes a solid white point, and it is the edit you still see at 60 px.
  • 3 Red eye is the smallest edit with the biggest effect: one red dot where the target was, and the drawing starts looking back at you.
  • 9 puts both together. It is the most sinister version that is still plainly the original. Two edits feels like the limit.
