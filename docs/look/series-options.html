Roboteer wave P: the Series screen, the shop and the campaign board

Brief v5 (docs/brief-v5-progression.md) replaces the season with five Series of four matches and one continuous Level bar, and adds a shop. Design system section 8 still holds: one hero per screen, at most two boxed panels, navigation that is not the hero is a text link, money is a sentence, and the words tier, circuit, season and bracket never appear. Two options each below; the one I recommend is marked and is the one built.

Option S1 · The card, with the Level bar over it
Option A of the menus board, grown a Level bar. The top row keeps the five Series as one line of words with the full-game wall in it; the Level bar takes the full width underneath, where the words above it have already taught you which Series you are in. The one panel is the next match, FIGHT inside it, Garage / Repair / Shop as links. The four matches plus the next Series sit under the card as a five-slot strip that opens the campaign board.
  • Continuous with the screen the owner already approved: the same hero in the same place, one new horizontal band.
  • Both progress tracks read in one glance and neither is a panel: Level as a rule, the Series as words.
  • Risk: the strip is five cells in 438 pt, about 84 pt each. Opponent names fit at 11 pt; a long one scales down.
Recommended, and built.

Option S2 · The strip gets the full width
The Series strip moves out of the column and runs the whole screen. The five Series words are dropped: the strip's last cell names the next Series instead, and the campaign board is a link. Each match cell gets about 160 pt, so "Match 3 · Hob Nail / next, The Grinder" fits at full size with no scaling.
  • Fixes the one thing wave V found on this screen: four to five cells in a 438 pt column is tight and truncates.
  • The Level bar shares the top row with the chevron rather than owning a band, so the screen is two bands, not three.
  • Cost: two full-width horizontal bands sit across the machine's head, which is the picture the owner asked to keep clear. And losing the five-word line removes the one place the whole campaign is visible without a tap.
Honest alternative. It reads the matches better and the campaign worse.

Option H1 · The counter
One list, the whole width, grouped under kind headings. The five kinds are a line of words at the top that jump the list, not tabs, so every group is always in the same list and scrolling past a heading tells you where you are. Each row is name, weight, the same pips the builder shows, the price in the scrap colour, and one word for what you can do: BUY, OWNED, LEVEL 35, or the price in danger when you cannot afford it. The current Level and scrap are one sentence at the top, not two bugs.
  • 822 pt of row means the pips fit at full size beside the name with no wrapping, and a 45-part catalogue reads as five short lists rather than one long one.
  • The action word is in the same column on every row, so the eye scans one column to find what it can buy.
  • Risk: about seven rows visible, so browsing the whole catalogue is a scroll of six screens.
Recommended, and built.

Option H2 · List and detail
A narrow list on the left, the chosen part written out on the right with one BUY. The part gets a blurb, its Level, and the arithmetic of the purchase spelled out before you commit.
  • The only option that explains what a part does, which matters for the thirteen parts brief v5 adds.
  • One hero on the screen, which is the rule, and buying is deliberate rather than a word at the end of a row.
  • Cost: two boxed panels and half the list width, so only four rows are visible and each purchase is two taps. Buying eight parts after a good Series becomes sixteen taps and a lot of reading.
Its detail panel is worth stealing later: the shop row could carry the blurb on the focused row only.

Option C1 · The grid
Five rows, four columns, the whole campaign on one screen with no scrolling. A Series is a row, a match is a cell with the opponent and what happened, the final is the last column and says so in the header rather than on every cell. Won cells are lit, the next one glows, locked Series are dimmed as a whole row with the reason once on the left.
  • Answers "how far through the game am I" in one glance, which is the only reason this screen exists.
  • Twenty cells at about 175 x 42 pt: the opponent at 11 pt and the result under it both fit.
  • After the campaign the same cells carry "Replay", so the screen has a second life instead of being a museum.
Recommended, and built.

Option C2 · The climb
Five columns read bottom to top, so the campaign looks like a climb. The same twenty cells, turned ninety degrees.
  • The vertical read gives the finals a position of honour at the top of each column.
  • Cost: 160 pt cells truncate opponent names, the "bottom to top" order has to be explained in words, and a grid you read up is the opposite of how every other list in the game reads.
  • Left-to-right also matches the Series strip on the Series screen, which is the thing this board expands.
Drawn to be sure. C1 wins on legibility and on consistency with the strip.

Notes

Every string here is the string the screen will print: plain sentences, no em dashes, no exclamation marks, and the words tier, circuit, season and bracket appear nowhere. "Locked" cells say what unlocks them once, on the Series, not twenty times. The wall between Series 2 and Series 3 is the existing full-game offer: tapping a locked Series raises the same unlock screen the game already has.
