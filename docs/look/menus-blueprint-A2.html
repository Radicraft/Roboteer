Blueprint menus A2

The owner picked direction A (Blueprint overlay) with one change: "the menu options work, but the visuals around the machine aren't quite right... we need more going on, dry ice etc. perhaps even an arena." So A's panels, rows, plates, leader lines and type (Saira Stencil One, Saira Semi Condensed, B612 Mono) are kept exactly, and only the 3D scene behind them is redesigned. Four stagings, each on the same five screens (main menu, builder, shop, Series board, match result), with the main menu again at a larger size.

Every backdrop is a real Godot render from the game's own assets, captured today in a Godot window (Forward+, high tier, 1912 x 880) with Arena/qa/menus_a2_capture.tscn: the live workshop stage with the proving slice Tinribs, and the Coliseum, the kit's entrance gate and the workshop venue around it. Everything on top is A's live HTML at 956 x 440 pt, shown here at 874 x 402. The scene is darkened and softened under each plate, and every leader's dot is moved to the part's projected position in that render, so the callouts still land on the flipper, the wheel or the armour they name. Nothing on the screens has a stroked edge or a skewed accent.

Showroom, turned up

The stage the game already has, pushed hard: a dry ice bank rolling off the plinth, four moving heads sweeping through real haze behind the machine, a hard cold rim and embers drifting up through the key.

CharacterPremium and controlled, the closest to Gear.Club's showroom: the machine is the only warm thing in a cold, moving room.
Trade-offStill a showroom. It is livelier than A but it does not say robot combat until the face-off; the embers need rationing so they never sit on a plate.

Recommended

The gate

The machine on its mark in the mouth of the entrance gate before the walk-out: lit from inside the tunnel, dry ice spilling over the steel, the gate's lamp strips and pyro going up either side.

CharacterAnticipation: the walk-out moment, every menu a second before the doors open. The most cinematic silhouette of the four.
Trade-offThe gate is built alone from the kit (no arena wall in the shot), so the crowd is only implied, and the tunnel haze greys the close screens if it is turned up any further.
4

Workshop pit at night

The machine standing in the workshop's test pit after hours, the tool wall and cabinets behind, halogens on tripods, a welder's sparks at the bench and a blueprint of the machine thrown across the floor from a projector in the roof.

CharacterTies the staging to direction A: the plates on screen and the drawing on the floor are the same blueprint. Warm, lived in, the team's own place.
Trade-offThe least spectacle of the four and the workshop reads busier than a stage; the projected drawing is clearest on the main menu and softer on the close screens.

Recommendation

2, In the arena. It is the only staging that answers the owner's own words ("more going on, dry ice, perhaps even an arena") on every screen: crowd, truss, pyro and the gate are already built, tiered and in the pack, and on older phones it falls back to the showroom.

Take two things from the others. From 1, the moving heads sweeping through haze behind the machine on the close screens, where the bowl is out of frame. From 4, the projected blueprint on the floor under the plinth: it is the one element that ties the scene to the blue plates on top of it.

Rules to hold when it is built: the pyro fires on arrival at a screen and on a win, never on a loop; the crowd and the grade stay behind the plates (the darkening under them is part of the look, not a fix); and the stage key stays the brightest light on the machine, so it reads orange, not purple.
