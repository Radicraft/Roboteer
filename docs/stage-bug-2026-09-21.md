# The slow stage bind and the boot loading screen (2026-09-21, finished 2026-09-24)

## What the owner saw

After wave P (13 new part models, two PBR armour sets, ten paint jobs, new FX and audio) the
builder and the menu opened on an EMPTY turntable, and the machine appeared some time later.
The menu also read as inactive for that beat. Owner's ask: "put a loading screen upfront on game
start, that way everything in game has a smooth experience".

## Cause

The one Godot view sits behind every SwiftUI screen, so the first frame the player sees is a full
3D frame: the workshop stage with its lights and dry ice, the arena behind it, the FX tree, then
the machine bound onto the turntable. Two costs land in that first frame, both on the main thread
inside one `Main::iteration()` (so SwiftUI in front cannot redraw either):

1. **Reading.** The first bind of each part loads its `.glb`, its material slot `.tres` files and
   (for a painted machine) the paint overlays from the pck. Measured headless on this Mac, cold:
   53 part models 110 ms, 7 material slots 24 ms, the whole walk of everything the game can open
   (37 scenes/environments/arena materials/FX textures, 53 models, 21 materials and paint
   overlays, 62 sounds) 330 to 340 ms.
2. **Drawing.** Every material in the frame has its render pipeline compiled and its textures
   uploaded the first time it is drawn. Headless has no renderer, so this cannot be measured
   here. It is the part that grew most with wave P (PBR sets, detail overlays for paint, 14 new
   particle emitters), and the CPU numbers above (first bind 76 ms cold, 2.6 ms warm; 91 ms from
   nothing to a machine on the turntable) are far too small to explain a visible wait on their
   own. So the working diagnosis is: the wait on the phone is pipeline compilation and texture
   upload in the first frames. **This is not yet measured on a device** (see "Owed").

## What was built

**`Arena/render/warmup.gd` (`ArenaWarmUp`)**, a child of the fight root (not of the fight world,
so a fight rebuild cannot take it away). Idle until the shell sends `preload_begin`; then, sliced
by a time budget (90 ms per frame on a RenderingDevice renderer, 1500 ms on GL Compatibility
where a frame is the expensive unit):

| Phase | Loading-screen line | What it reads or does |
|---|---|---|
| stage | Setting the stage | `workshop/stage.tscn`, `fight/fight_world.tscn`, `bot/bot.tscn`, HUD, controls, camera rig, `fx/fx.tscn`, the three environments, the bus layout, every `arena/*.tscn` and `arena/events/*.tscn`, `assets/materials/*.tres`, `fx/textures/*.png` |
| parts | Rolling out the machines | every part in the catalogue manifest (bosses and FX meshes included, 53) and every `PartVisuals.MATERIAL_FILES` slot |
| paint | Mixing the paint | every paint overlay (albedo and normal) the livery tab can apply |
| sound | Checking the sound | every stream in `AudioMix.SOUNDS` plus anything else in `res://audio/` (62) |
| lights | Warming the lights | the off-screen DRAW (below) |
| done | Ready | `preload_done` with `ms`, `read_ms`, `draw_ms` and counts |

Everything read is held in a static array for the life of the process, so the cache cannot drop
it. Directory walks use `ResourceLoader.list_directory`, which sees through the pck's
`.import`/`.remap` files; checked against the exported `arena.pck` (same counts as the source
tree: 37 scenes, 53 parts, 14 paint files, 62 sounds).

**The warm draw.** A 96x96 `SubViewport` with its own world, a camera using `stage_env.tres`,
and a spot, a directional and an omni light, all with shadows. Batches of 8 on the phone (all at
once on GL Compatibility) are stood up and left for 2 frames each: every part bound with its
RUNTIME materials through `PartVisuals.bind` (the previous draft drew the raw glTF materials,
which would have compiled the wrong pipelines), one chassis per paint job (10) with
`Livery.current` set and restored inside the call, and one `GPUParticles3D` per
`FxEmitters` flavour (5). 68 draws in all. Hard cap 20 s: past it the walk finishes and anything
undrawn compiles lazily, as before. Skipped automatically when there is no renderer (headless
display or an empty adapter name, i.e. the dummy driver).

**The shell.** `ArenaHost` sends `preload_begin` right after `arena_ready`, after the pending
workshop config, so the player's own machine is bound first and is already standing on the
turntable (and being drawn, behind the scrim) while the rest is walked. `RootView` builds the
menu from the first frame but keeps it at opacity 0 until `preloadDone`, with `LoadingView` on
top; the handover is a 0.45 s cross-fade.

**`LoadingView`** (Broadcast, quietest form): the live stage under an `ink` scrim that eases
from 88 % to 30 % as the real fraction rises (so the stage lights come up with the work), the
title ROBOTEER in display type, and one plain sentence (the phase line above; "Opening the
workshop" before the arena has answered). No spinner, no bar, no percentage, no accent colour,
no edge strokes, no skewed rules. The fraction is files read plus batches drawn over their total,
never a timer.

**Never trapped.** A give-up timer (40 s) is armed at launch, before the engine answers, and
re-armed when `preload_begin` is sent; if it fires the menu shows anyway. `preload_begin` is
idempotent in the arena (a second one is answered with `preload_done`, never run twice).

**QA flags.** `-skipLoading` skips it. `-autoFight`, `-autoTest`, `-autoCareerFight` and
`-autoSendFight` imply the skip, so a fight never counts down behind the scrim. All other
screen flags (`-autoBuilder`, `-autoWorkshop`, career flags, `-open*`) navigate underneath at
once and are revealed when the walk ends, on the warmed frame. A headless or dummy run
(`-godotDriver dummy -godotMethod dummy -godotDisplay headless`) never shows the loading screen
and never sends `preload_begin`; the arena side would also skip the draw on its own.

## Timings measured (this Mac, headless Godot 4.6, 2026-09-24)

| What | ms |
|---|---|
| 53 part models, cold | 110 |
| 7 material slots, cold | 24 |
| stage.tscn load + instantiate + enter tree | 15 |
| first `show_bot`, cold | 76 |
| second `show_bot`, warm | 2.6 |
| `show_bot` after the full walk | 18 to 56 (varies run to run) |
| the whole walk, reading only (headless skips the draw) | 330 to 340, over 4 frames |
| the draw PATH forced headless (68 stand-ups, no compile) | 52, over 8 frames |

Headless cannot compile a pipeline, so none of these include GPU work.

## Tests

`Arena/tests/warmup_test.gd` (32 checks): the walk covers every part, material slot, paint
overlay, scene, arena and sound; progress only moves forward and ends at exactly 1.0 on
"Ready"; `preload_done` exactly once, and again on a retry; headless with `{}` finishes without
drawing (the no-hang guarantee); everything is in the resource cache afterwards; the draw plan
covers every part, paint and flavour; and with the `force_draw` seam the draw path itself stands
up all 68 jobs, restores `Livery.current` and frees its viewport.

`Arena/tests/_warmup_draw_probe.gd` (helper, not in the suite) measures the draw and the first
six stage frames with and without the walk; it needs a renderer, so it is run windowed and
silent (`--audio-driver Dummy`). It was NOT run for this change: windowed runs and simulators
were off limits while the owner was at the Mac.

## Owed on device

- **Time the walk on the iPhone 18 Pro Max**: the `warm up done in … ms (read …, draw …)` line
  and `first playable frame at … ms since process start` in the `spikeLog` (Console, subsystem of
  the app). Expect read well under a second; the draw is the unknown.
- **Confirm the fix**: cold-launch, open the builder, and check the machine is there on the
  first frame with no stall. If a stall remains, the pipeline variants differ from the stage's
  (e.g. SSR/SSAO/volumetric fog on the stage environment in Forward+); the next step would be to
  render the warm batches inside the stage's own world instead of a private one.
- **First launch vs later launches**: Godot keeps a pipeline cache on disk, so the second launch
  should be much faster than the first; both numbers are worth recording.
- Run `_warmup_draw_probe.gd` windowed on the Mac (Forward+ and `gl_compatibility`) when the Mac
  is free, and a Simulator screenshot pass of the loading screen through to the menu.
- Memory: everything read stays resident (about the size of the unpacked models, sounds and
  textures). Check `memoryAtReadyMB` / five-second sample against the previous build.
